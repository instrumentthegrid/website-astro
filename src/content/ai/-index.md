services:
  - title: The AI - Power Relationship 
    description: The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and digital services is driving an unprecedented wave of data center development across the US. New data centers are being deployed in regions with a specific combination of transmission capacity, reliable power, favorable regulatory environments, and proximity to major fiber networks. Importantly, these large, unpredictable loads connect to both the transmission and distribution sides of the grid, introducing significant reliability risks and power quality challenges.<br/><br/>Traditional enterprise data centers support corporate IT operations and have moderate, predictable power demands. However, the newest generation of AI and high-performance computing (HPC) data centers, representing the fastest-growing data center segment, are characterized by extremely dense computing loads, rapid fluctuations in power demand, and unprecedented energy consumption. Large facilities represent some of the largest individual loads on a utility system, so sudden changes in computing workloads cause rapid shifts in power demand. Concentrated clusters of data centers (like Northern Virginia, Texas, the Midwest, Arizona, and Georgia) not only place significant stress on local substations, but their broader regional transmission networks.

  - title: AI Harmonics and Grid Reliability Risks 
    description: Harmonics are voltage waveforms that occur at frequencies that are integer multiples of the fundamental 60 Hz US grid frequency. Rather than consuming power in a smooth sinusoidal pattern, non-linear loads create distorted waveforms that interfere with the normal operation of electrical equipment. As the concentration of data center load increases, harmonic levels are now starting to negatively impact both utility infrastructure, customer-owned assets, and grid reliability.<br/><br/>Transformers, switchgear, motors, generators, protection systems, and sensitive electronic devices experience degraded performance, nuisance tripping, inaccurate measurements, and premature failure when exposed to excessive harmonic distortion. More alarmingly, harmonics have caused catastrophic, cascading generation failures, making data centers a previously unimaginable risk to bulk and distribution networks.

  - title: Proactive Harmonic Distortion Response
    description: Harmonic studies are a critical part of the data center interconnect and grid planning process; however, data center sub second fluctuations at MW scales present an entirely new risk to the US grid. “Harmonic management” is no longer a long-term strategic planning exercise. By necessity, it is rapidly becoming a highly sophisticated, automated response platform operating at millisecond monitoring and management speeds…everywhere and all-the-time.<br/><br/>By proactively addressing and managing harmonics in real-time, utilities, developers, and system operators can support digital growth, while preserving the reliability, efficiency, and long-term health of the electrical grid.
